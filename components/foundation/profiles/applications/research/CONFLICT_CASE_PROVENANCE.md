# Research Conflict Case Provenance — 0.2.0 Stable

Research question: `Does bilingual experience produce a replicable executive-function advantage over monolingualism?`

The scenario deliberately contains conflicting independent literature rather than selecting only evidence favorable to one answer.

Support:

- Bialystok et al. (2004), *Bilingualism, aging, and cognitive control: evidence from the Simon task*, DOI `10.1037/0882-7974.19.2.290`.
- Ware, Kirkovski & Lum (2020), *Meta-Analysis Reveals a Bilingual Advantage That Is Dependent on Task and Age*, DOI `10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01458`.

Opposition / replication challenge:

- Paap & Greenberg (2013), *There is no coherent evidence for a bilingual advantage in executive processing*, PMID `23370226`.
- Lehtonen et al. (2018), *Is bilingualism associated with enhanced executive functioning in adults? A meta-analytic review*, DOI `10.1037/bul0000142`.
- Dick et al. (2019), *No evidence for a bilingual executive function advantage in the nationally representative ABCD study*, DOI `10.1038/s41562-019-0609-3`.

The Profile does not decide which publication is “true”. It records provenance, support/oppose mass, source independence, validation status and unresolved conflict. Literature triangulation is explicitly non-strong validation.
